# SENTRAVYN Safety, Content and Verification Policy

## 1. Safety-first rule

SENTRAVYN must not create a false impression of certainty. If information is unknown, stale, incomplete or unverified, say so clearly.

Official local emergency instructions take precedence over general SENTRAVYN guidance.

## 2. Information classes

Every displayed item must be distinguishable as one of:
- Official source content.
- SENTRAVYN-reviewed explanatory summary.
- Third-party data.
- User-submitted information.
- Derived calculation.
- Unverified or stale information.

Never visually imply that a summary is itself an official order.

## 3. Place verification

A map point is not a verified safe location.

For shelters, require documented evidence of:
- Responsible verifying authority.
- Shelter classification and intended hazard applicability.
- Structural or facility assessment where relevant.
- Accessibility and available facilities.
- Capacity/occupancy evidence where published.
- Inspection/verification date.
- Expiry or revalidation policy.

Do not publish a shelter as verified without the required evidence and authorised approval.

Hospital location, operational status, emergency department availability and capacity are separate claims requiring separate evidence.

## 4. Nuclear and radiological incidents

Differentiate:
- Nuclear detonation.
- Nuclear power plant accident.
- Radiological dispersal incident.
- Lost or damaged radioactive source.
- Other radiological exposure events.

Do not merge their instructions.

Use current authoritative public-health and emergency-management guidance. For example, CDC public guidance for a radiation emergency emphasises getting inside, staying inside and staying tuned, with appropriate sheltering inside a substantial building. The exact advice must remain linked to the applicable official source and jurisdiction.

Do not:
- Predict fallout paths from unvalidated wind data.
- Calculate safe zones without validated authoritative models.
- Recommend potassium iodide as a universal remedy.
- Certify a building as a radiation shelter from map data.
- Independently instruct users to evacuate or remain in place contrary to official orders.
- Present artistic ASCII artwork as a real explosion or prediction.

## 5. Other hazards

Create independently reviewed guidance for earthquake, tsunami, flood, cyclone, wildfire, industrial/chemical, conflict and public-health hazards. Do not reuse generic instructions across hazard types.

## 6. AI restrictions

AI may:
- Translate approved guidance.
- Summarise source documents with citations.
- Explain terminology.
- Help search reviewed content.

AI must not:
- Declare an event confirmed without authoritative evidence.
- Generate official alerts.
- Issue evacuation orders.
- Certify shelters or hospitals.
- Infer safe routes through hazards.
- Override official instructions.
- Convert uncertainty into certainty.

Every generated summary must retain links to the underlying authoritative source and disclose that it is a summary.

## 7. Alert validation

Validate:
- Source identity and authenticity.
- Schema and required fields.
- Issued/effective/expiry times.
- Geographic geometry.
- Severity, urgency and certainty semantics.
- Update/cancel references.
- Duplicate identity.
- Provider freshness and known outages.

Malformed or suspicious records must enter quarantine/review, not public publication.

## 8. Stale data

Set freshness policies per provider and data class. Display last-updated timestamps and stale labels. Never silently present stale information as current.

## 9. User reports

Treat user submissions as unverified until reviewed. Prevent public submissions from changing official alert or shelter status without authorised approval.

## 10. Review and accountability

Safety guides require a named responsible role, qualified review process, authoritative references, version history and review date. Keep an auditable record of changes and approvals.
